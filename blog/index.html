Perpetual Impasses

November 10, 2026

I kind of enjoy debating people on religious and political topics, although I tend not to anymore. Ruefully, I tend to stay within my own circle when talking about subjects of that nature because I've grown to dislike conflict (I don't know if that's necessarily a good thing...) Although when I was more argumentative with those of differing viewpoints, I would get really into the weeds.

For example, I was and am still very passionate on the subject of abortion. I believe it to be objectively wrong to intentionally take the life of a child in the womb no matter its stage of development. I have yet to encounter anybody in person that disagrees with this, likely due to my previously mentioned isolation from dissenters, but in past years I would stupidly participate in multi-day arguments on Twitter on the subject of abortion.

Throughout these arguments in political meme comment sections, I noticed that disagreements usually came down to a single root factor. For abortion, the root factor is usually whether or not an unborn child has any moral value. I, obviously, believe that they do and that belief is rooted in my faith. For others who may not share my faith it becomes a more complicated situation. The morals of a secular person are rooted in what society determines to be good.

The current secular view on morality is that everyone should be treated with love and respect. That's easy to get behind, but falls flat when speaking on the subject of abortion. Since secular individuals do not believe in any sort of soul or inherent value in conception, it's easy to permit abortion. The logic is thus: the child is not sentient, therefore he cannot be hurt by it. It's simply a clump of cells to them, and some even call the baby a parasite. Another reason people permit abortion is based on this argument. They truly believe it is not yet human. So then you may ask yourself, "At what point does it become human?" and that leads to a whole host of other arguments.

I digress, if two parties cannot agree that a child inside the womb is a human with inherent value, then there is a perpetual impasse. We Christians cannot prove the existence of a soul and the pro-abortionists will never be able to define what is human and what is not. This is not a problem exclusive to abortion. Almost every topic in popular culture nowadays deals with arguments that have their roots in impasses.

Another is transgenderism and gender ideology. Advocates of this idea claim that someone can be a man while simultaneously "identifying" as a woman. This is possible to them because they hold the belief that sex and gender are different concepts entirely: While sex is biological, gender is a social construct created by society. While part of that statement is true, their supposed concept of "gender", which is just a stand in for the concepts of masculinity and femininity, emerges itself from sex and not from society. If it did, then we would see widescale differences between the male and female archetypes throughout the diverse societies of the world, and yet we don't. This is also not to mention that when simply asked what a man or woman is, purporters of gender ideology cannot give an answer. They only circularly define men and women as men and women.

Despite all the evidence that suggests otherwise, those who believe in the gender spectrum believe gender to be a modifiable aspect of the psyche that is sometimes incongruent with biology. Because I cannot look into someone's mind and know for certain that they weren't born with the mind of the opposite sex, this cannot be proven false. There is our impasse.

People wonder why we can't all just get along. We can't get along because the problems that our culture has convinced us to care so much about are rooted in unfalsifiability. What can we do about this? Well, we need to hold our ground. Use evidence as a weapon. Keep noticing. Follow the teachings of Christ. Look to our forefathers. Things will probably get worse, but the gates of Hell shall not prevail against us.

Let's Note

October 26, 2026

Pinterest is an excellent resource for images. The algorithm, and it's rare I praise something like this, has a very appealing functionality. Unlike algorithms on Instagram and YouTube which are annoying and intrusive, Pinterest's just works the way you want it to. If I click on a photo of a dog, it shows me more photos of dogs. If I click off that photo of a dog and click a photo of a train, it shows me more photos of trains. No bullshit. A while ago I was browsing through Pinterest when I found an image of an interesting looking laptop.

I came to find out that this laptop is a Panasonic Let's Note CF-R5 from the year of our Lord 2006. Panasonic is in a lot of different industries including photography, television, home appliances, and even hair dryers, but it's not often that you see Panasonic laptops outside of police cruisers and factory lines. Yes, Panasonic is well known for their line of Toughbook laptops but these Let's Notes are a bit more obscure to the West. This is because they were primarily sold in Japan as high-end business laptops.

The CF-R5 is just one of many revisions of the Let's Note and you'll be surprised to find that the newer models look almost identical to the old ones. As you can see from the image, the R5 model features a unique circular touchpad, a design choice that has followed even into the latest model, a 5:4 display, and is chock full of IO. The font on the keyboard, the green power indicator, the various status LEDs, the tasteful thickness of it -- I love it so much. As soon as I found some more pictures of this model, I needed to have one to add to my collection.

I went to Ebay and searched for the laptop. To my dismay, there were very few results for the R5. There are plenty of newer models on there, but the older notebooks that I'm looking for are rare to come by on Ebay in good condition for a fair price. I did some research and found out that you can get these types of laptops generally cheaply on Japanese auction sites, but you'd need to go through a third-party service in order to get them shipped to the US, which usually involves paying tons of fees. No thanks. I did find one listing that looked promising. It was not the R5, but it was from the time period I was interested in. Unfortunately, the seller mentioned that the battery does not accept any charge. The batteries are user-serviceable, but good luck getting your hands on a loose, working battery for one of these laptops if you're outside Japan.

Anthropomorphization

August 24, 2026

In the last post I wrote about the pets I had growing up and the pets I have now. I love my pets and animals in general. Animals are the second most complex things in the universe and they deserve a worthy place on the ladder of most interesting things ever, but I can't help but cringe at the amount of value that some people assign to them. I am a Catholic, so I believe that all living things are created by God and for that reason should be treated with respect. Caring for animals is a noble thing to do because they are pure creatures unlike us. That is the key, however. They are not us. They were not created in God's image.

Although animals deserve respect, I think a line should be drawn. I've noticed a trend in people around the ages of 25 - 40 -- they tend to treat animals like humans. I was recently talking to someone who will not be named and they referred to the owner of a dog as the dog's "mom." Not only is this weird but it indirectly dehumanizes children and downplays the role of a parent. If someone assigns the role of "mother" to the owner of an animal, what does that say about that person's understanding of the role of a mother? Is a mother just someone who pours food in a bowl and pick up shit? Well, no, I suppose that's too simplified. There's much more that goes into taking care of an animal. Animals need socialization and attention, just like children. But children are human, made in the image of God. They're aware of themselves and others and have a concept of love that's based on more than kibble and rubber toys. Mothers don't just throw food in a bowl in front of their daughter, pat her head, and call her a good girl 70 times a day. A mother is THE defining role model of any young girl, as is the same for a boy and his father.

The job of a parent is to teach their children how to do good and how to avoid evil and to respect themselves and others. The most you can teach a dog is how to sit on command, which it will only do if rewarded with food. This is obedience, not respect, and certainly not love.

Cat Person

August 6, 2026

I had 3 dogs growing up. One was a poodle, and the other two were miniature dachshunds. I loved those dogs, but dogs don't live as long as humans. Maverick, the poodle, died of natural causes around 2017. The dachshunds got to the point where they were living a miserable lifestyle. My parents had mercy on them and put them down around the same time. While the dogs were still alive, my sister brought a cat home from college that she named Pan. He was just a kitten at that time. Growing up in a house full of loud canine pets, it was weird to only have one lazy cat hanging out in the living room.

Eventually, that same sister brought home another cat she had named Kiki. Kiki was owned by my sister's landlord. The house my sister lived in was sold while she was still a tenant, and the landlord simply left Kiki there. My sister's roommate was not too fond of animals it seems, as she actively tried to indirectly kill Kiki by depriving her of food. My sister, having a passion for cats in particular and not being able to take care of her herself, opted to take Kiki from her home under the rented house's patio to our house, where she lived out the rest of her days in comfort.

Kiki was very old. How old, we didn't know, but it was very obvious by her slow movement and strained snarls that could hardly be excused for meows. We would occasionally bring her inside the house, but it was obvious that she preferred our screened in sunroom, where she could bask in the Florida sun. Pan, at first, was hesitent of Kiki. Pan is and always has been an inside cat. To him, Kiki was a member of an uncontacted wild tribe of cats, unfamiliar in her demeanor. Kiki could not care less about the presence of Pan. Pan would hiss at Kiki and run away from her if she got too close, but after a couple weeks he eventually warmed up to her. It wasn't long before they were basking in the sunlight together.

Kiki too eventually succumbed to the passage of time, and we buried her outside next to the dogs. For a couple more years, Pan was the sole feline inhabitant of our household. That was until about July of 2022, when my mother and brother found the smallest cat I've ever seen in my life walking alongside Hyde Park Road. It literally fit in the palm of my hand. We estimated that it was about 2 weeks old, judging by pictures we looked up on the internet. We quickly ran to PetSmart and got some milk replacement and a kennel for it. We made some other observations and determined it was a boy. We named him Hyde after the road we found him on. It was obvious from his inflated stomach that he had some sort of worms, so we took him to the vet on the same day we found him.

To our surprise, the veterinarian told us he was a she. Hyde was such a masculine name, so we switched her name to Heidi, keeping the lore-accurate name but compensating for her gender. Heidi is about 4 years old now and still a petite cat for her age. We're guessing she was the runt of the litter and was kicked out of wherever it was her mother was keeping the rest of the kittens. It took Pan a little longer to adjust to Heidi for some reason, but they are best buds now. They play every morning in the kitchen and Pan can even be seen cleaning her from time to time. Heidi loves to be in the screened in patio for some reason. I think she likes how cool the cement is when it's hot outside, so that's usually where she'll be found if they're let out into the patio. Pan, instead of sleeping on the cat beds or several other padded places in patio, decides to sleep on the top of the couch, almost falling between it and the wall.

I've spent almost as much time having cats in the house as I have dogs. I grew up with dogs, but I entered early adulthood with cats. After 8 years I kind of forgot what dogs were like. Some of my family members have dogs, but I'm never around them for more than a couple hours. Recently, a lady from my church asked me to dogsit for her for two weeks while she went to visit family up North. I obliged, because who could turn down being paid to spend two weeks alone at a house with a pool? I paid the price. I rediscovered that dogs are needy as hell, almost never shut up, and cleaning up their excrement is a process I never want to go through again. This dog is about 4 years old, and weighs no less than 50 pounds. The dog either wouldn't eat all day or scarfed down the food she was given in about 5 seconds. She would stare out the back door of the house for at least half of the day, watching for something that wasn't there, and if she noticed that a breeze swayed a few leaves in the bushes she would go apeshit. I ended up having to close all the blinds in the house to prevent her from waking me up in the middle of the night.

One morning, at about 5:30 AM, I was awoken by what I perceived to be something falling on me. I opened my eyes to see that the dog, shivering and panting aggressively, had pounced onto me in bed. I immediately assumed that the dog was dying and began comforting it, imagining what I would have to tell my church friend when she got back from vacation. It was then when I heard distant thunder outside, and the dog stopped panting for a moment, jumped off of me, and hid under the bed. It was in fact not dying, but intensely mortified of the thunder. I eventually was able to coax her out of my bedroom with shredded cheese and I gave her some anxiety meds that the her owner had left me. That did essentially nothing and the dog proceeded to cower on my lap in the living room.

Upon returning home, I was welcomed by meows from Pan and Heidi. They rubbed their faces on me for a few seconds before laying down in their usual spots. That night it stormed again, and I saw heidi silently watching the moths outside the back porch circle the light. Pan was asleep on top of the couch in the sunroom, and I was reminded of Kiki because that's where she used to sleep. I sometimes wonder if he remembers her.

I've already made some friends, most of which are women. Yes, nursing school is attended by primarily women, but I'm okay with standing out from the crowd. Now onto the meat of what this post is about: I'd heard all my life that colleges love to push certain narratives in the curriculums they choose to teach, that there are underlying political and social ideas being subliminally taught behind the scenes. This was part of the reason I didn't initially go to college right out of high school, I didn't want to deal with that shit. To my surprise, the first 3 classes I took were free of any sort of political banter. Granted, they were biology, psychology, and english composition, but I thought maybe they'd find a way to shove some political garbage in there.

Psychology was on the edge, but never really expressed any radical ideas. I felt good about everything by the end of my first term. I was happy with the way I was being taught, although I didn't really enjoy have 2 of my 3 classes online. My second term started recently. I'm now taking anatomy and physiology, effective communication, and sociology. I was eager to jump into my classes. I opened up my sociology book to the first chapter and... Joe Biden was looking back at me.

On week two of sociology, our prompt was asking how we would feel if our culture was replaced by another country's culture. Computers

July 25, 2026

Most people have one subject or hobby that they know the most about, and mine is computers. I've used many computers throughout my life. The first one I can remember using was a Windows XP desktop located somewhere in the house I lived in as a child, but after that we were an Apple household. My parents bought an iMac around 2011 and that became the family computer, back when having family computers was still a thing. As I grew up, I inherited and purchased several Macbooks. I've had 2 Macbook Airs and bought a Macbook Pro about 2 years ago that I still use daily. I'd say that Macbook Pro is one of the best purchases I ever made.

I've had several desktop computers as well. I used the first paycheck from my first job to buy a pre-built Windows 10 PC. I used that thing all through high school before I bought a purple M1 iMac in 2021. Nowadays, it simply acts as a router for my current PC that I built around 2024. Most people build gaming computers, and this PC is capable of gaming, but that isn't its main purpose. It's more of an all-purpose computer. I use it to back up and organize old home videos and music, and I have an archive of all my favorite YouTube videos. It's the mothership of all my other computers. It's running Arch Linux with a highly customized version of XFCE.

I recently noticed this trend online of people glazing Lenovo ThinkPads for some reason, especially in the Linux community, and wanted to know what all the hubub was about. I researched some ThinkPad models and found one that resonated with me, the T440p. The T440p is highly modular. It has a socketed CPU, in some cases supports 32GB of RAM, a built in DVD drive, easily replaceable keyboard, trackpad, and display, a multitude of different IO, and supports librebooting. I went on Ebay and found one for parts and repair for about 30 bucks. It was missing its RAM and storage drive, but was in generally good physical condition aside from a cracked screen which I was going to replace anyway.

First I found a 1TB NVME drive in my room and cloned my Arch Linux desktop installation to it, then snatched the SSD from inside and put it into the ThinkPad. Then I found a 1920x1080 14" IPS panel online and replaced the terrible native 1366x768 panel which also had a massive gash in it. I found a compatible backlit keyboard on Ebay to replace the dirty worn out one that it came with, and put in 16 GB of DDR3 RAM. I am not quite comfortable replacing the CPU yet, because I don't know that much about processors. I can only tell you that it has an i5 in it. I just set up a minimal Arch Linux install with Openbox and I'm using Xfce4-panel for a basic info bar at the top displaying battery life, time and date, wifi connection, and volume. So far it does everything I want it to. It doesn't like when I try to run Half Life 2 though...

Change of Heart

February 22, 2026

I worked construction for about 4 months. It wasn't exactly what I expected to have been doing, but I enjoyed it to some extent. I made a couple friends, learned some valuable skills, but in the end it was just another phase of my life. I got laid off at the end of December.

I got home the day I was laid off and began stressing about the thought of being a bum. The possibility of living with my parents until the age of 30 was a major concern. I thought and thought, "What am I to do?". The easiest route to success and independence was the military. Great benefits, respect from all, training in any field I wanted all for free, and all for minimal consequence; or so I thought. I mentioned in another blog post that I had met with an air force recruiter and it didn't work out very well. Not long after that, I met with a Navy recruiter. I walked into the recruiting office to find an environment much different than what I had encountered at the air force. Unique wooden desks dotted the walls and long, precise posters hung in the main office encapsulating everything the Navy believed in. It was much better.

I met with this Navy guy a couple times and while a bit awkward and slightly socially offputting, he was a nice dude. He answered any questions I had and seemed like a really genuine guy. I proceeded with the process of joining the Navy.

I was at work one Friday, eating animal crackers with the children at the school, when I got a text message from the Navy recruiter. He told me I was scheduled to go to MEPS the following Tuesday. I knew it was gonna be soon, but I didn't think it was gonna be that soon. I had planned to go home that night to research a little bit about MEPS because I was completely blind to the details. In that moment, something clicked. A very subtle anxiety kicked in. I wasn't really enjoying myself anymore. I tried my best to be amicable with the children but, to their frustration, I separated and decided to watch them from a distance at recess, not wanting my gloomy demeanor to interrupt their thoroughly looked-forward-to time on the playground.

From that moment on doubt was an ever-present idea lingering in the background of my thoughts on the subject of military life, and it only grew stronger as the days passed. Tuesday came faster than I would've liked, and I found myself standing in front of a recruiting center with a small backpack at my feet. There was no one else there, which was strange to me at the time. From what I had read online, I was supposed to be boarding a shuttle with countless other recruits, but there I stood alone. Eventually my shuttle driver arrived and I found out that I was the only person riding with him, which he found considerably humorous.

It was a 3 hour drive to where I would be staying for the next day. I tried to read a book that my father had given me, but gave up when I found out it was far outside my reading level, and opted to listen to the Fox News broadcast my driver had tuned into. I listened to it for an hour and half, but I couldn't tell you anything they had said. I was so lost in my thoughts.

We arrived 3 and a half hours after our departure because my driver, who I had learned was named Gary, needed to stop and use the bathroom. The base where I was to be initially processed was mostly barren fields with occasional runways that looked seldom used. A few institutional looking buildings lined some of the roads. Long story short, I was there to take a test and then I was shoved back onto a different shuttle and taken to a hotel 15 minutes away. On the way I met a guy I don't remember the name of. He was 18, still in high school, and obviously grew up on a rural farm or something as was evident by his accent. When we arrived at the hotel we attended a short meeting with a couple other recruits, were given dinner, and instructed to go to our rooms and stay there until early the next morning.

I'm hesitant to tell any more about this process because I remember them telling us at one point not to speak about it to anyone, which I found strange, but I don't remember if they were referring to something specific or the entire process, so I'm just gonna skip to the end of the day. I was in a waiting room, which I had been in for 2 1/2 hours at that point with no one exiting or entering the room I was supposed to go into, and the Chinese boy came and sat beside me. I was reading a New Testament pocket book they were giving out for free in the waiting room. I noticed he was interested in what I was reading, so I stood up, grabbed another one, and gave it to him. He flipped through it and began telling me about how religion was considered something to be mocked in China, and that he found it fascinating that people in the United States unironically worshipped a God.

He went on to explain that ever since he had arrived a couple years prior he was interested in Christianity. He explained that he had been to several protestant services and found them to be lackluster and wanted to attend a Catholic mass, but felt unworthy. Being a Catholic, I explained to him that everyone is worthy to attend the Mass and encouraged him to visit my church. (He was from my town) I began explaining the basics of Catholicism to him before I was suddenly called into the meeting room. I told him I'd be right back, but I never saw him again.

Again, I don't want to speak on the processes we went through, so I'm going to heavily abridge the events of the last hour or two I was there. Essentially, I was conned into signing up for something I did not want. I don't want to say I was lied to, because technically I wasn't, but there was a fair amount of deception at play from multiple parties. I was told by countless people not to listen to or trust recruiters but I guess I wasn't careful enough. The drive home that night was hopeful at first because I didn't yet understand the situation I was in. I called my boss, my parents, and texted back some people that had tried to contact me while they had my phone locked away, and then started to research what I had been assigned to do. My heart sank as I read through the details. It was not what I expected and not what I wanted to do for 8 years.

I got home at around 8:00 PM that night and that point I was suffering from a substantial amount of anxiety. I was fuzzy on the details of the past couple of days because, honestly, things were not explained to me well at all, possibly on purpose. In order to bring this post to a close because I'm sick of thinking about all of this again, I'll just say that I am not joining the Navy. I was thrust into an environment that was completely foreign to me and, at first, I was okay with that. I was told by my boss that I had to bear a bit of discomfort in order to grow and although that may be true, I didn't want to be part of a community of people that deliberately manipulated me, not to mention that when things set in I found I really did not want this. I love my church and my home. I love my parents and my friends. I love the humid Floridian air and the embrace of the children when I arrive at work. I don't need to learn to march in synchronicity and disassemble a rifle in order to be happy. Some may call me a coward or a hypocrite and they might even be right, but... well, I don't have an argument for you.

Special Ops

January 22, 2026

I found myself in an Air Force recruiter's office recently. I walked up to the door, gave it a knock, and hoped someone would answer. After waiting for about 10 seconds with no answer I turned back to my car, assuming they were closed. But, to my surprise I heard a door chime behind me just as I was reaching for my car door. A tall man with a shaved head was peering out the doorway. He was wearing air force cammies and big steel toed boots.

He asked me if I had an appointment and I said no and that I just wanted to chat with someone about the Air Force. Another recruiter approached me and said he'd help me out. I walked inside rather confidently only for him to hand me a form that asked a bunch of personal questions. I relunctantly answered them, gave him back the form, and he told me that I could come back the following day to have a more in-depth conversation. I agreed and promptly left.

The next afternoon I returned with a manila envelope filled with identification papers and an over-rehearsed, pre-planned conversation ready in my head. I knocked on the door, and the same recruiter greeted me with a firm handshake. We sat down at his table and I waited as he finished writing an email. The office was exactly what you'd expect. Beige office dividers with posters of planes and people saluting the flag plastered on every wall. Interestingly, the recruiter I was meeting with was the only one not wearing cammies that day. He was just wearing a blue collared shirt and some khaki pants.

"So," He started, "Did you watch those videos I sent you?" (He had sent me a promotional video about the special ops, and a podcast discussing the same subject)

I told him I watched the promotional video and skimmed through the podcast. He enthusiastically praised the podcast for a bit, before beginning to tell me more about the special ops. I quietly listened until I could find a way out of that conversation. I began telling him about where I went to school and what was going on in my life, and then pulled out my laptop to show him some of the air force jobs I was looking at online. He quickly shut me down. I'm gonna summarize here; he essentially told me I don't get a guarantee on what I going to do in the air force, or in the military in general.

"Well, yeah I understood that to some extent, but do I really have no choice in the matter?"

I told him about my love for computers and how I worked to get a start in the IT field but came up short. I explained that I was hoping I could do that in the air force.

"You can do IT work anywhere." He said, scolding me. "Why do you need to do it in the air force? Is that the only reason you want to join, to work on computers?"

At this point, the conversation had gone far from what I had rehearsed in my head, and I kind of didn't know what to tell him. He then started asking me questions that I would later learn were probably rehearsed, and tried to convince me to join the special ops. I looked at the videos he was showing me on his computer of people jumping out of planes and running through fields in the Middle East and I just gave him a hard No. I did not want to run through hostile territory, or get tortured in an arabian prison. Lost to Time

January 9, 2026

I like to collect images that I find on the internet. I love images that you can throw into a reverse-image search engine and no results come up. It's almost as if you've found something rare. I'm somewhat of a digital hoarder, which I'm willing to admit because it's significantly more sanitary than being a physical hoarder. I have all my files neatly organized on each machine I own and I spend an ample amount of time keeping it that way. There's few things more distressing to me than a cluttered filesystem.

When space gets low, I take the files I wish to save (which is most of them) and upload them to the NAS attached to my home network. I'm on my third NAS now, the first two are not all the way filled but are sort of specialized for Apple device backups, so I keep them unplugged and stored on my bookshelf. The process of backing up files is an annoying and long one, but necessary in my opinion. The practice of not backing things up is how things get lost, and that's the last thing I want. Among the endless drove of random images and old Arch Linux ISOs are videos of my friends and I from high school that I want to play for my son one day. Also, who knows, maybe something you have downloaded will become lost media.

Lost media is an interesting phenomenon because in the digital age we're used to receiving whatever we want, whenever we want it. So naturally, when we find out that something is not immediately available to us on the internet in any shape or form, we tend to get desperate. An example of a piece of lost media is the 2006 Roblox client. Roblox has actually existed since around 2004, but didn't exist in its semi-modern state until around 2006. Back then, users could visit the Roblox website and download an executable that would install the game binaries from Roblox's servers. This installer and the files it downloaded are essentially lost to time. Although Roblox is one of the biggest MMOs of modern times, the same couldn't have been said for it in 2006. Roblox is estimated to have had about 11,000 registered users in 2006. That may seem like a lot but it pales in comparison to today's 380 million monthly active users.

This is a big part of the reason the client is lost. Relatively, not a lot of people ever downloaded it, and the ones who did are now much older and very unlikely to have saved the files associated with it, and even if they had, the mechanical nature of hard disks guarantees they will die someday and the files with them. Some have said, "Well, Roblox likely has a backup of these sorts of things, we can ask them to release it, and even if they don't we can rest assured that the files are out there somewhere, albeit not on the clearnet." Well, actually, Roblox has said they don't have it either. This makes things a little more interesting because some pieces of lost media are only lost because they're being gatekept by the institution that created them in the first place. This is not the case with the 2006 Roblox client. It has simply slipped through the cracks of archival, and may be fully lost. If there is a copy of it out there somewhere it's on an old, decrepit hard drive sitting in someone's garage, possibly partially corrupt. Do NOT trust these corporations to preserve something that you love. You will be sorely disappointed every time.

So download things you like and back up your files, not only for your sake but for everyone's. If you see a YouTube video you like, despite what you might think, it's really easy to download it. You can use yt-dlp, a command line program, JDownloader2, a graphical program, or one of the many web-based video downloaders. Also, find any old hard drives you have and transfer the files to a USB stick. Check if any old pieces of software you have are uploaded to the Internet Archive, there's a ton of lost software out there. Hell, dust off those VHS tapes in your attic and use OBS to digitize them, God knows what missing episodes of TV shows might be on there.
